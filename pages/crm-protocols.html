Section 2 — Staffing Contingency Protocol
Last reviewed: Saved
Three failure modes. Each one has a numbered response sequence. Owner decides means this cannot be delegated. Act within the time window listed — do not wait and see.
Scenario A — Technician Gives Notice (Planned Departure) Notice Given
  • 1
    Same day — Owner decides: Is this recoverable (counter-offer) or irreversible? Make the call within 24 hours. Do not enter a multi-week "wait and see" loop. If recoverable: go to step 2. If not: go to step 3.
  • 2
    Counter-offer window (optional): One conversation. One offer. If declined, accept the departure and proceed to step 3. Do not negotiate beyond one attempt.
  • 3
    Within 48 hours of confirmed departure: Freeze new bookings beyond the last day the tech will work. Do not book jobs you cannot staff. Update the schedule view to reflect actual capacity.
  • 4
    Notify booked customers proactively: If any jobs fall after the tech's last day, contact those customers immediately. Offer reschedule or cancellation. Do not let them find out the day of. Script: "We have a staffing change and want to make sure we can still take great care of you. [Offer reschedule options]."
  • 5
    Start replacement pipeline same week: Post the role. Tap personal network. Contact any formerly-vetted applicants. Owner handles all vetting — do not skip this step because of optimism about the timeline.
  • 6
    Exit procedure: Confirm all CRM accounts are deactivated on the last day. Keys, equipment, uniforms returned. Final pay processed per agreement. Get written confirmation of last day.
Scenario B — Tech Calls In Sick (Day-Of) Same-Day Crisis
  • 1
    Immediately (within 15 minutes of call-in): Owner pulls today's schedule in the CRM. Identify which jobs can wait 1–2 days vs. which are time-sensitive (no-power situations, commercial customer, same-day SLA promised).
  • 2
    Decision — can owner self-perform? If owner holds a valid license and has the tools: self-perform the highest-priority job. Mark remaining as rescheduled. If no: go to step 3.
  • 3
    Call your backup list: Maintain a list of at least 2 licensed electricians who have agreed to take overflow work. Call them in order. Do not text — call. This list must exist before launch.
  • 4
    Customer communication — every affected job, within 2 hours of sick call: Call or text each customer. Offer same-week reschedule as first option. Offer full cancellation with no fee as second option. Do not make them wait for a call that never comes. Script: "Good morning — I'm Joshua Vickery. We have a tech out sick today and need to reschedule your appointment. I can get you in [date/time] — does that work?"
  • 5
    Update CRM: Reschedule or cancel bookings in the scheduler. Do not leave "completed" jobs on today's schedule that weren't done. Keep records clean.
  • 6
    If this happens more than 2 times in 30 days: Treat as a staffing reliability issue. Owner decides whether to add a second tech or restructure the scheduling model. Do not keep absorbing it.
Scenario C — Technician Loses License or Legal Authority to Work Critical / Immediate
  • 1
    Immediately upon learning: That tech does not perform any electrical work under the Vickery Electric TECL until status is resolved. This is non-negotiable. Working with an unlicensed tech under your TECL is your legal exposure.
  • 2
    Within 24 hours — Owner verifies: Check TDLR directly. Do not rely on what the tech tells you. Get the facts from the regulatory body.
  • 3
    Freeze all jobs that require licensed work: If the tech's license was the only licensed authority on a job type, freeze those bookings. Do not perform the work with an unlicensed tech, regardless of how the tech or customer pushes.
  • 4
    Contact an employment/business attorney if license was revoked for cause: Understand whether there is a disclosure obligation. Do not assume — get a professional answer within 72 hours.
  • 5
    If license is restorable: Work with the tech on the reinstatement timeline. Keep them on in non-licensed roles (driving, materials, customer-facing tasks) only if the agreement allows it. Do not pay idle-tech rates indefinitely — set a clear deadline.
  • 6
    If license is not restorable: Treat as a resignation. Follow Scenario A steps 3–6. Begin replacement pipeline immediately.
Section 3 — Refund & Dispute Handling Policy
Last reviewed: Saved
Refunds are executed via the invoice system's change-order deduct flow. Open the invoice in the CRM, add a change order with type "Deduct," enter the refund amount and description. This automatically adjusts the balance and creates a record. Below defines when to do that and what language to use.
Situations That Warrant a Refund or Adjustment
RefundWorkmanship defect — reported within 30 days
If work performed by Vickery Electric fails due to our error (loose connection, wrong circuit, fixture falls from box we installed) within 30 days of job date, we return to fix it at no charge. If we cannot fix it within 5 business days, we issue a partial or full refund for that line item. Amount is pro-rated based on scope of failure.
RefundNo-show by Vickery Electric — we missed the appointment
If we fail to show for a confirmed appointment and did not contact the customer at least 2 hours in advance, customer is owed a $50 credit toward their next service, applied automatically. No questions asked.
Partial RefundScope mismatch — we over-billed for work not performed
If a customer was billed for a line item that was not completed (e.g., we quoted 3 fixtures but only installed 2), the unperformed work is deducted from the invoice as a change order. No argument. Fix it immediately.
Situations That Do NOT Warrant a Refund
No RefundCustomer changed their mind after work began
Once the tech has opened walls, pulled wire, or removed existing fixtures, labor cost is earned. We will stop work at that point and charge for work completed to date. Customer is responsible for any additional cost to restore the prior state if they want work halted.
"I understand this isn't what you expected. Once we opened the wall and pulled the wire, that labor is complete — I'm not able to refund work that's been done. We're happy to finish the job at the quoted price, or stop here and charge for what we've completed today."
No RefundPermit delays or inspection failures not caused by our work
Permit processing time is outside our control. If an inspection fails due to pre-existing conditions in the customer's home (not our installation), additional work to correct those conditions is billable. We will explain this in writing before performing additional work.
No RefundCustomer complaints about price after work is complete
The price was provided before work began. If customer accepted the quote and work is complete as scoped, the price is owed. We do not negotiate post-completion prices under pressure.
"The quote you approved was $[X]. The work is complete as described. I'm not able to reduce the price after the job is done, but I'd be happy to talk through any specific concern you have."
No RefundCustomer-supplied materials that fail or are incompatible
If a customer provides a fixture, device, or component that fails or doesn't fit their existing wiring, we are not responsible for the failure. We will inform the customer of incompatibility before installation if identified. If we could not reasonably identify it before installation, the labor is still owed.
Written Dispute Process (Disputes Over $300)

For any dispute involving more than $300, the following process applies before any refund decision is made:

  1. Customer submits concern in writing (text or email) stating what went wrong and what resolution they expect.
  2. Owner responds within 2 business days with either: (a) agreement to refund with amount and timeline, or (b) explanation of why refund is not warranted, with supporting detail.
  3. If customer disagrees with response, Owner schedules a phone call within 3 business days. One call, documented.
  4. If still unresolved after the call, the matter is escalated to small claims court or mediation per the service agreement. We do not pay dispute settlements to avoid conflict — we pay them when we are wrong.
Section 4 — Weekly Business Review Process
Last reviewed: Saved — Every Monday before 9 AM
Five steps, every Monday before 9 AM. Use the Weekly Pulse page for the data. This is not optional when the business is running — it's how you know if you're winning or bleeding before it's too late to act.
1
Check Revenue Collected Last Week
Open Weekly Pulse. Look at "Revenue Collected This Week" (paid invoices). Compare to your baseline target.
StatusWhat it meansAction
$2,000+On track for a $100K+ annual run rateNo action — keep the pipeline full
$1,000–$1,999Below pace — check pipeline and booking rateReview leads from last week — what quotes didn't convert? Follow up on 2+ day old quotes.
Under $1,000Pipeline problem or execution problemOwner decides: is it a marketing gap, a scheduling gap, or a capacity problem? Address the root cause this week, not next.
2
Review Aged Invoices — Anything Over 10 Days Unpaid
Weekly Pulse shows any invoice sent more than 10 days ago that hasn't been paid. Each one gets a direct action this week.
AgeAction
10–20 daysSend one follow-up SMS: "Hi [Name] — just checking in on your Vickery Electric invoice for $[X]. Let me know if you have any questions." One message, no more.
20+ daysCall directly. Not text. If no answer, leave a voicemail and send one final SMS. If still no response by day 30, Owner decides: write off, collections, or small claims.
Customer says they won't payDo not perform any additional work for this customer. Document the conversation. Consult the refund policy — if we owe them something, settle it and close it.
3
Check Next 7 Days of Scheduled Jobs
Look at Upcoming Jobs on the Weekly Pulse. For each job this week:
  • Is the address correct and reachable? (Service area check if in doubt)
  • Does the tech have all required parts/materials for that job type?
  • Has the customer received a confirmation? (Should be automatic — verify in CRM)
  • Is there any job flagged as needing a permit? If so, has it been pulled?
If any job has a gap, fix it before Wednesday. Do not let problems surface the morning of the job.
4
Scan Expenses and Margins
Look at expenses logged this week. Two questions:
  • Anything over $200 that wasn't expected? — Tool replacement, supplier price jump, extra material run. Log it. If it's recurring, adjust pricing or markup.
  • Are jobs being invoiced at or above the quoted price? — If change orders are consistently going up (you're adding work), that's good. If they're consistently going down (you're giving discounts), investigate why.
5
Make One Decision Before Closing the Review
Every weekly review ends with one concrete decision. Not "think about it" — a decision with a deadline.
TriggerDecision to make
No jobs completed last weekIs it a marketing problem, a conversion problem, or a scheduling problem? Pick one and fix it this week.
3+ invoices past 15 daysStart collections process on the oldest. Don't let receivables grow.
Revenue above $3,000 two weeks in a rowConsider adding a second booking slot, second tech, or adjacent service.
Revenue below $800 two weeks in a rowOwner personally makes 5 outreach calls to past customers this week. Not email. Calls.
Tech called in sick twice in 30 daysHave the staffing reliability conversation this week per the Contingency Protocol.
