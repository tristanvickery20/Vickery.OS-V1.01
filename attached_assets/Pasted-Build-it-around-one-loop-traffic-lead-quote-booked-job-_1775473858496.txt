Build it around one loop:

traffic → lead → quote → booked job → 5-star review → repeat job / referral

If your CRM can own that loop, you can compete hard even as a small shop.

I’d build your “elite engine” in 4 stacked layers:
	1.	Acquisition engine — SEO, GBP, LSA, direct traffic, referrals
	2.	Conversion engine — instant response, quote follow-up, booking

	4.	Compounding engine — reactivation, referrals, repeat work, content reuse

The mistake would be building “marketing” as a tab with campaigns and texts.
You need to build a revenue engine that starts at first click and ends at repeat business.

3. Review engine

This is one of the biggest advantages you can build.

What elite looks like

Not just “send review request.”

It means:
	•	send at the right moment
	•	use the right channel
	•	send from the right number/email
	•	stop automatically when review is left
	•	route unhappy customers away from public damage
	•	make responses fast and consistent
	•	use reviews in SEO and conversion pages

Review workflow I’d build

After job completion:
	1.	mark job completed
	2.	wait a short delay
	3.	send SMS review ask
	4.	if no click, send one follow-up
	5.	if review comes in, stop
	6.	create task to respond publicly
	7.	save review text to testimonial library
	8.	tag review by service and city
	9.	surface it on matching service pages later

What to store
	•	ask_sent
	•	ask_channel
	•	click_detected
	•	review_received
	•	star_rating
	•	review_text
	•	service_tag
	•	city_tag
	•	technician_tag
	•	public_response_status

Smart twist

Make the CRM ask for an internal satisfaction score first if you want service recovery — but be careful not to build anything that feels manipulative or like sketchy gating. The safe operational version is:
	•	internal feedback form available
	•	complaint workflow
	•	public review request still handled cleanly and fairly
	•	no shady “only happy people get the link” logic

Review response engine

You should also build a response queue:
	•	new review detected
	•	suggested response draft
	•	owner approve/edit
	•	mark responded

This helps speed and consistency.

What you should be thinking about while coding

Think like this:

“Can I tie this to revenue?”

If not, it is probably not a top priority.

“Can this be automated after one human action?”

Example:
job marked complete → review flow fires automatically

“Can I reuse this asset?”

Example:
review → testimonial → page content → ad copy → text snippet

“Can I see this by source?”

If not, you will not know what to scale.

“Can I make this impossible to forget?”

If not, it should become a queue or automation.
