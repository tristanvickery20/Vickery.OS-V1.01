Got it. Treat this as a gap-audit checklist for cloning Frappe HR behavior inside your CRM, not a pitch.

One important note first: Frappe HR is documented as a full HR/payroll product with 13+ modules, and a lot of what feels like “features” are really connected screens plus rules between them. So the right way to audit your CRM is not just “do I have this page,” but also “does this page trigger the next thing automatically?”  ￼

1) Main HR home / workspace

This is the top-level landing area. In Frappe HR, users navigate into employee management, shift & attendance, leaves, recruitment, lifecycle, performance, payroll, expense claims, reports, and settings from organized module areas rather than one giant mixed CRM screen.  ￼

Your audit questions

* Do you have a dedicated HR sidebar/nav separate from sales/jobs?
* Can a manager get to attendance, leave, payroll, recruiting, and employee records in 1–2 taps?
* Are admin/setup screens separated from day-to-day operator screens?

2) Employee list screen

This is your master people list. Frappe HR maintains a full employee database and supports related org records like department, branch, designation, employee grade, employee group, health insurance, and org chart.  ￼

Must-have features

* searchable employee list
* active/inactive status
* branch / department / designation filters
* employee profile entry point
* hire/exit state visibility

What it should connect to

* attendance
* leave
* payroll
* appraisals
* advances / claims
* transfers / promotions / separation  ￼

Gap test

* Can one employee record act as the source of truth for every HR workflow?
* Or do you have employee data duplicated across multiple modules?

3) Employee profile screen

This is one of the most important screens. Frappe HR’s employee records tie into contact info, salary/payroll records, attendance, leave, appraisals, lifecycle changes, and reporting. The docs also note hierarchy-based access using the employee’s “Reports To” chain.  ￼

Must-have tabs/sections

* personal/contact info
* employment info
* department / branch / designation / grade
* manager / reports-to
* shift assignment
* leave balances
* salary structure assignment
* claims / advances
* appraisal history
* onboarding / separation history

Logic

* manager hierarchy should control visibility of subordinate requests
* changing department/designation should affect approvers, staffing, or template matching where applicable  ￼

Gap test

* When you open one employee, can you actually manage the whole employee lifecycle from there?

4) Department / org structure screens

Frappe HR has dedicated screens for Department and Organizational Chart. Department setup can store leave approvers and expense approvers, and org chart shows hierarchy by employee, designation, image, and name.  ￼

Must-have screens

* Designation list/detail
* Employee Grade
* Org Chart

Must-have logic

* hierarchy visibility

Gap test

* Does your org structure actually drive workflows, or is it just display data?

5) Attendance list screen

Frappe HR has a standard Attendance screen where attendance is recorded daily per employee with statuses like Present, Absent, On Leave, and Half Day; future dates cannot be marked.  ￼

Must-have features

* date + employee + status
* optional shift link
* submit/finalize state
* monthly attendance reporting

Logic

* attendance can feed payroll working-day calculation, depending on payroll settings
* half-day handling should affect pay math if payroll uses attendance  ￼

Gap test

* Is attendance just informational, or does it change payroll and leave behavior?

6) Bulk attendance / upload screens

Frappe HR includes Employee Attendance Tool for marking multiple employees at once, plus Upload Attendance and Attendance Request flows. The attendance tool lets users filter by department and branch for a given day.  ￼

Must-have screens

* bulk attendance entry

Why it matters
Without bulk tools, HR staff waste time doing one employee at a time.

Gap test

39) Hidden interconnections you absolutely need

These are the important “why it works” pieces:

* Department can define default leave and expense approvers.  ￼
* Reports To hierarchy controls subordinate visibility for some records.  ￼
* Shift Request approval can create Shift Assignment automatically.  ￼
* Shift Schedule Assignment can keep generating repeating future shifts.  ￼
* Shift Location plus geolocation can block out-of-zone check-ins.  ￼
* Employee Checkin logs attach to shifts, and off-shift logs can be excluded from auto-attendance.  ￼
* Leave Policy Assignment drives leave allocation behavior, including earned-leave schedules.  ￼
* Staffing Plan limits allowed Job Openings.  ￼
* Closed Job Openings block new applicants.  ￼
* Job Applicant can be generated from inbound email.  ￼
* Onboarding and Separation are template-driven task systems, not just status fields. Separation submission creates a project/tasks and completes when activities complete.  ￼
* Payroll can calculate working days from either attendance or leave, depending on settings.  ￼
* Salary Structure Assignment is effective-date based, so history is preserved.  ￼
* Payroll Entry runs in bulk by org filters.  ￼
* Employee Advance and Expense Claim are linked but distinct states/workflows.  ￼

40) The fastest way to audit your CRM

Use these five labels against every screen above:

* Missing entirely
* Exists but isolated
* Exists and connected
* Exists but weak UX
* Exists and production-ready

Then prioritize in this order:

1. Employee profile + org hierarchy
2. Attendance/check-in + shifts
3. Leave engine
4. Recruiting to onboarding
5. Payroll settings + structures + payroll run
6. Expenses/advances
7. Performance + reports  ￼

The reason for that order is simple: those are the screens that create the biggest web of downstream logic in Frappe HR. If those are weak, the rest becomes manual busywork. That prioritization is my inference from how the documented modules depend on shared employee, attendance, leave, payroll, and lifecycle data.  ￼
