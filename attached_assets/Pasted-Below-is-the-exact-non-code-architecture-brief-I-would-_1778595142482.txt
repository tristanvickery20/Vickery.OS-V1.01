Below is the exact non-code architecture brief I would give Replit/Codex for the CRM marketing system.

Core research takeaways first:

Your market is big enough for a 6-truck residential electrical company, but not big enough to waste leads. The Beaumont–Port Arthur metro is about 398,733 people, made up of Hardin, Jefferson, and Orange counties.  ￼ Orange County has about 38,572 housing units and a high 75.2% owner-occupied rate, which is good for residential service, repairs, lighting, panels, fans, generators, and small upgrades.  ￼ Jefferson County adds about 110,841 housing units with a 62.5% owner-occupied rate, giving you volume in Beaumont/Port Arthur/Nederland/Groves/Port Neches.  ￼

Owned & Operated’s marketing doctrine lines up with what you need: prioritize demand capture before brand fluff, do not spread budget thin, measure the whole funnel, and max out working channels like GBP, LSA, PPC, and SEO before dumping money into broad awareness. Their marketing episode specifically calls out Google Ads, GBP/GMB, LSAs, and SEO as non-negotiable demand-capture channels, and warns against billboards/brand spend before the company has enough lead flow.  ￼ They also say the fix for marketing confusion is tracking numbers, CRM data, and measuring from call to close, not just cost per lead.  ￼

Your local competitors are mostly selling “experience, licensed, reliable, call us.” Lone Star Electric SETX claims 20+ years of experience, 100+ happy customers, broad service areas, financing, generator installs, panels, troubleshooting, rewiring, and repairs.  ￼ Christopher Electric pushes 24/7 service, free estimates, since 1984, BBB, chamber membership, and long-tenured technicians.  ￼ Savant is leaning hard into residential electrical plus generators/Generac, including generator maintenance and hurricane preparedness content.  ￼ Your opening is not “we have more experience.” Your opening is faster, cleaner, priced upfront, trackable, easier to book, better follow-up, and more transparent.

Give Replit This: Vickery Electric Marketing OS Architecture Brief

Project Goal

Build a Marketing Command Center inside the Vickery Electric CRM.

This is not a generic marketing dashboard. This system must help Vickery Electric grow from a 1-truck / 2-person launch company targeting 100 leads per month into a future 6-truck / 12-person company without losing track of lead quality, call response, booked revenue, gross margin, reviews, referrals, reactivation, or contractor accountability.

The CRM must become the source of truth for:

* Where every lead came from.
* Whether the lead was qualified.
* Whether the customer saw a price.
* Whether the customer booked.
* Whether the job was completed.
* Whether the job made money.
* Whether the customer left a review.
* Whether the customer referred someone.
* Whether that marketing channel deserves more spend.

The system should be built around this doctrine:

Lead → sale → fulfillment → review → referral → reactivation → channel ROI.

Do not build vague marketing screens. Build a measurable marketing operating system.

Strategic Reason

Vickery Electric’s main competitive advantage is not age, size, or legacy reputation.

The advantage is:

1. Upfront pricing
2. Instant estimator
3. Fast response
4. Online booking
5. Photo-gated scope control
6. CRM-enforced follow-up
7. Clear job assumptions
8. Tight source tracking
9. Review/referral loop

The local market already has experienced electricians. The CRM must help Vickery compete by being easier to buy from and easier to trust.

Marketing Doctrine To Build Around

Use the Owned & Operated home-service marketing model:

1. Demand capture before demand creation

The CRM should prioritize channels where customers already need an electrician:

* Google Business Profile
* Google Local Services Ads
* Google Search Ads
* Organic SEO
* Service pages
* City pages
* Referral traffic
* Past customer reactivation

Do not build the CRM around generic social media posting, broad branding, billboards, or vanity awareness first.

Those can come later.

2. Do not spread budget thin

The CRM should help the owner identify which channel is working and push deeper into it before adding more channels.

Example logic:

* If GBP is producing profitable jobs, improve GBP/reviews/photos.
* If LSA is profitable, increase LSA budget.
* If PPC is profitable, add more service-specific landing pages.
* If a channel produces leads but not booked revenue, flag it.
* If a campaign produces low-margin work, flag it.
* If a city creates too much drive time, flag it.

3. Track booked revenue, not just leads

The CRM must not treat “lead count” as success.

Every campaign should track:

* Leads
* Qualified leads
* Quotes
* Booked jobs
* Completed jobs
* Revenue
* Gross profit
* Gross margin
* Cost per lead
* Cost per booked job
* Cost per completed job
* Return on ad spend
* Lost reasons
* Average ticket
* Review conversion

4. Reviews are a marketing asset

Google reviews must be treated as a growth system, not a nice-to-have.

The CRM should trigger review requests after happy paid jobs and track which customers left reviews.

Reviews affect trust, GBP strength, LSA performance, and conversion.

5. The call center / lead response process is part of marketing

Missed calls and slow replies are marketing waste.

The CRM must track:

* First response time
* Missed calls
* Callback status
* Unanswered form submissions
* Quotes not followed up
* Needs-review leads not reviewed
* Booking drop-off

Review System Architecture

Trigger review request only after:

* Job completed
* Invoice paid or payment collected
* No complaint flag
* No safety/quality issue open

Review workflow:

1. Job completed.
2. Tech marks customer mood: happy, neutral, unhappy.
3. If happy and paid, CRM schedules review request.
4. If no review after 24 hours, reminder.
5. If no review after 3–5 days, final reminder.
6. If review received, customer becomes referral-eligible.

Review fields:

* Review requested
* Review requested date
* Review platform
* Review received
* Review rating
* Review text
* Review source job
* Review response needed
* Review response completed

⸻

Referral System Architecture

Referral should happen after the review ask, not at the same exact moment.

Referral workflow:

1. Customer completed and paid.
2. Customer happy or left review.
3. CRM sends referral message or creates tech handoff task.
4. Referral code generated.
5. New referred lead enters CRM.
6. Referral connected to original customer.
7. Reward becomes payable only after referred job is completed and paid.
8. Owner approves payout.
9. Payout marked paid.

Referral statuses:

* Generated
* Shared
* New lead received
* Job booked
* Job completed
* Reward pending
* Reward approved
* Reward paid
* Rejected

Lead Source
→ Tracking
→ CRM Lead
→ Qualification
→ Instant Quote or Review
→ Booking
→ Job Completion
→ Invoice/Paid
→ Review
→ Referral
→ Reactivation
→ Channel ROI

The system should answer these owner questions every day:

* Where did my leads come from?
* Which leads are worth chasing?
* Which channels are producing booked jobs?
* Which campaigns are wasting money?
* Which services are most profitable?
* Which cities are worth expanding into?
* Which calls were missed?
* Which quotes need follow-up?
* Which customers should be asked for reviews?
* Which customers should be asked for referrals?

That is the exact architecture: a marketing command center tied directly to operations, revenue, reviews, and profit.
